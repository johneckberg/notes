## 1. Learning and The Neuroscience of Learning (CH 2)

### Judger and Learner Attributes
This dichotomy defines an individual’s cognitive approach, focusing on **control and certainty** (Judger) versus **curiosity and adaptation** (Learner).

| Attribute | Focus & Mindset | Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Judger** | **Controlling, Reactive, Limited**. Focuses on justifying, being right, and the past. | Asks: "Who is at fault?", "What is wrong?", "How can I prove I am right?". |
| **Learner** | **Flexible, Adaptive, Plentiful**. Focuses on curiosity, being open, and the future. | Asks: "What can I learn?", "What worked?", "What is possible?", "What am I responsible for?". |

### Bloom's Taxonomy for Educational Goals
A hierarchical framework that classifies learning objectives into six cognitive levels of complexity, moving from basic recall to complex creation:

1.  **Remember:** Recalling facts and basic concepts (e.g., Define, List).
2.  **Understand:** Grasping the meaning of concepts (e.g., Explain, Summarize).
3.  **Apply:** Using information in new or novel situations (e.g., Execute, Solve).
4.  **Analyze:** Breaking information into parts and examining relationships (e.g., Compare, Contrast).
5.  **Evaluate:** Making judgments based on criteria and standards (e.g., Appraise, Justify).
6.  **Create:** Combining elements to form a new, coherent, or original whole (e.g., Design, Formulate).

## 2. Self-Disclosure & Trust (CH 3 & 4)

### Self-Disclosure vs. Self-Description
* **Self-Description:** Disclosure of nonthreatening information (e.g., age, major).
* **Self-Disclosure:** Revealing **significant personal information** about one's thoughts, feelings, and wants that exceeds expectations and is not easily apparent.
    * **Benefits:** Aids in **reducing stress and tension**, improves communication, strengthens relationships, and **increases team productivity**.

### Johari Window
A model used to map and improve mutual understanding between individuals. The goal is to enlarge the **Open Area** by reducing the Blind and Hidden areas.

| | Known to Self | Unknown to Self |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Known to Others** | **Open Area (Arena):** Mutual understanding. Grows with disclosure and feedback. | **Blind Area:** Reduced by **Feedback** from others. |
| **Unknown to Others** | **Hidden Area (Façade):** Reduced by **Disclosure** from self. | **Unknown Area:** Potential for discovery. |

### Five Elements of Trust Figure
Trust is an aspect of relationships that is earned and easier to destroy than build. The key elements are:

1.  **Integrity:** Honesty, sincerity, and the ability to honestly disclose.
2.  **Competence:** Knowledge and ability; knowing one's strengths and limitations, and offering/seeking help when needed.
3.  **Consistency:** Conformity with previous practice; doing what one says they will do.
4.  **Loyalty:** Faithfulness; supporting associates both within and outside their presence.
5.  **Openness:** Willingness to share ideas and welcome new ideas. (Note: This is cited as the **Most important trust factor for employees** in an organizational setting).

## 3. Stress & Productivity (CH 3 & 4)

### Managing Stress
Effective management involves cognitive and behavioral strategies. A key framework is the **Four A's of Stress Management**:
* **Avoid:** Unnecessary stressors (e.g., learning to say "no," avoiding certain people or topics).
* **Alter:** The situation (e.g., expressing feelings openly, being more assertive, compromising).
* **Adapt:** To the stressor (e.g., reframing problems, adjusting standards, looking at the big picture).
* **Accept:** The things you cannot change (e.g., seeking support, practicing forgiveness).

### Time Management & Productivity
Time management is a primary tool for stress reduction. Techniques include:
* **Prioritization:** Keeping a "to-do" list and prioritizing tasks by urgency and importance.
* **Setting Boundaries:** Establishing clear work-life boundaries (e.g., not checking work email in the evening).
* **Minimizing Distractions:** Turning off email alerts or phone notifications to maintain focus.
* **Delegation:** Delegating responsibilities where appropriate.

## 4. Goals, Change, Strengths (CH 3)

### SMART Goals
A widely used framework for effective goal setting, providing clarity and direction:

* **Specific:** Clearly defined, addressing exactly what needs to be achieved.
* **Measurable:** Includes criteria to track progress and quantify success.
* **Achievable (or Attainable):** Realistic and possible given available resources and skills.
* **Relevant:** Aligns with personal, team, and organizational objectives.
* **Time-bound:** Sets a clear deadline, creating a sense of urgency and commitment.

## 5. Team Development, Values & Ethics (CH 3)

### Kohlberg's Moral Maturity
(Key Note: This is a major concept often used in leadership and ethics slides outside the core textbook content, as noted by the user.) This model outlines three levels of personal moral development:

1.  **Level 1: Preconventional (Self-Interest):** An individual follows rules only to **avoid punishment** and acts in their own immediate self-interest.
2.  **Level 2: Conventional (Societal Expectations):** An individual lives up to the expectations of others, fulfills duties, and **upholds laws**.
3.  **Level 3: Postconventional (Internal Values):** An individual follows self-chosen, universal principles of justice and right, seeking to **balance individual and common good**.

### Ethical Reasoning & Dilemmas
* **Ethical Dilemma:** A quandary where a person must make a choice between two or more conflicting moral options, or where the decision involves judgment rather than indisputable facts.

* **Utilitarianism (Teleological):** A consequence-oriented ethical view.
    * **Core Idea:** Decisions are judged based on their outcomes, striving for the **greatest good for the greatest number** of people.

* **Kantian Ethics (Deontological):** A duty-oriented ethical view.
    * **Core Idea:** Actions are inherently moral based on the **action itself being good** (a moral imperative), regardless of the consequences. Certain values like autonomy and individual rights are absolute.

## 6. Emotional Intelligence (CH 1 & 4)

### MBTI; Specifically Tendencies
The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is a personality framework based on four dichotomies (tendencies):

| Dichotomy | Preference 1 | Preference 2 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source of Energy** | **Extraversion (E):** Outward focus, learns by **doing/talking**, needs external stimulus. | **Introversion (I):** Inward focus, learns through **reflection/thinking**, needs time alone. |
| **Ways to Take in Information** | **Sensing (S):** Focus on the **here and now, facts, details**, and hands-on experience. | **Intuition (N):** Focus on **future possibilities, meanings, and patterns**, trusts "gut" feelings. |
| **Ways to Come to Conclusions** | **Thinking (T):** Values **logic and objective** analysis, critical, strives for what is fair. | **Feeling (F):** Values **compassion and harmony**, considers effects on people, empathetic. |
| **Ways to Interact with Outside World** | **Judging (J):** Values **organization, methodical**, decisive, and needs **closure** (prefers plans). | **Perceiving (P):** **Flexible, adaptive**, spontaneous, and prefers to **keep options open**. |

### Emotional Regulation
The ability to influence **which emotions we feel, when we feel them, and how we express or experience them**. It is a critical skill for leaders and team members to handle conflict, manage stress, and build trust.

* **Key Strategies (Cognitive and Behavioral):**
    * **Cognitive Reappraisal (Reframe Interpretations):** Changing how a stressful event is interpreted to focus on learning opportunities or a more positive perspective.
    * **Attentional Deployment:** Shifting focus away from the source of the emotion.
    * **Strategic Pause:** Taking a brief pause (deep breathing, stepping away) when emotions escalate to allow an individual to **respond rather than react**.
    * **Self-Awareness:** Recognizing emotional triggers and physical stress signals to interrupt negative cycles.
    * **Modeling:** Emotionally regulated leaders model composure and intentional responses, which fosters a climate of psychological safety and enhances team resilience.
